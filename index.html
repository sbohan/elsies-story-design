Brand / Our Story

Our Story

Elsie's Story is a charity built on love, legacy and action. It supports families, empowers communities and helps people make a difference in ways that truly matter. Everything we design starts here.

Our Girl Jen & David

A story that has become a community.

Elsie's Story began as a way for us to turn our love for her into something that could help others. What started as something deeply personal has grown into a community united by kindness and action.

Every fundraiser, every grant and every act of support helps us continue Elsie's legacy and create meaningful change in her name.

With love,

Jen & David

What we stand onLove, legacy, action

Three words from our mission. They are the test for every piece of design: does it feel loving, does it honour Elsie, and does it help someone do something?

Love

We lead with warmth. People come to us grieving, giving or asking for help, and the design should feel like a hand on the shoulder.

In designSoft blush and cream, handwriting, real names, "With love" sign-offs.
Legacy

Everything carries Elsie's name forward. We keep her story at the centre, told by the people who love her.

In designThe butterfly, the notebook, chapters and pages: a story still being written.
Action

Love turned into something useful: grants, projects, fundraisers. Every page offers one clear next step.

In designOne sketch button per section. Donate, apply, fundraise, follow.

The big ideaA scrapbook that everyone adds to

The brand is a family scrapbook. A spiral-bound notebook, cream paper, polaroids tucked in, notes written in pen and doodles in crayon. It was started by Elsie's family and now every supporter adds a page.

That's why nothing is too polished. The handmade parts are the brand. The discipline is in how they're arranged: clear type, generous space and one action at a time.

"Follow our story – written by you, shared by us."

With careShowing Elsie

Elsie is at the heart of the charity. Only use photos of her that Jen and David have approved, and keep them where her story is being told: Our Girl, Our Story and the founders' messages.

Never crop, filter, decorate or put text over her photos. Show her in a polaroid, as a family would, with her name or "Our Girl" written underneath.

Brand / Logo

Logo

Elsie's name, written by hand, with a pink butterfly landing on the E and "Charitable Trust" underneath in blush. It's the one thing in the scrapbook that's never tilted.

Three versionsPick the one that fits

The full logo signs off everything. The white logo is for the cocoa footer and dark photos. The butterfly is for small, square spaces where the full logo would be too small to read. Try each on paper and on cocoa.

The butterfly

Option fourThe animated logo

The same logo, written by hand. Every stroke was worked out from the logo itself, pixel by pixel: the pen writes each word in one flowing line, dabs the dot on the i and flicks in the apostrophe afterwards, and goes back for the t's downstroke last. As the pen lifts, the butterfly flies in and lands on the E, then "Charitable Trust" stamps in letter by letter. It ends on the exact static logo.

Use it for first impressions: the website home page, video intros and end cards, event screens and the opening slide of a presentation.

0.4–6.1sThe pen writes "Elsie's Story" stroke by stroke, slowing on the curves. The i's dot and the apostrophe go in after each word, and the t's downstroke comes last.
5.2–7.5sAs the pen lifts, the butterfly flies in on a looping path and lands on the E with a puff of hearts.
7.3–8.6s"Charitable Trust" stamps in, one letter at a time.
AfterThe butterfly flutters every few seconds, and when someone hovers over it.

Need it as a video file (MP4) for social media or events? Ask the team for an export. The page version and video version follow the same timings.

Give the logo space equal to the height of the butterfly on every side. Nothing else (text, photos or doodles) comes inside that line.

Brand / Colour

Colour

The palette comes from a desk: cream paper, a cocoa pencil, blush pink and the manila of a file folder. It's quiet on purpose. Photos and children's drawings bring the bright colour.

The palettePaint chips

Our nine brand colours, straight from the charity's own palette sheet. Pale Blush is our most used pink. Taupe and Paper Grey are the options for racer colours. Click a chip to copy its hex value.

Supporting colours from the website

Two more colours the website uses, for the footer and for tiny accents. Use them alongside the brand palette, never instead of it.

BalanceMostly paper

Pages are mostly cream paper with cocoa-grey writing. Soft tints set things apart, cocoa grounds the bottom of the page, and rose appears only in small marks: a focus ring, a heart, the word "love".

Readable for everyonePairings that work

Body text needs 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG 2.2 AA). The soft tints are too light for our usual ink colour, so on blush, oat and taupe always write in cocoa.

Pale Blush is a background, never a text colour: as text on white it's only 1.70:1. Where links or buttons turn pink on hover, they must also tilt or underline.

Brand / Illustration

Illustration

Crayon doodles that look like a child drew them, because some of them did. Then soft watercolour scenes with little pink hearts. Hover over the board to meet them.

Three kindsFrom doodle to scene

Use the simplest one that does the job. Doodles decorate. Watercolours tell a scene. Children's drawings are the real thing, credited by first name.

Crayon doodles

Blush, oat and ink only. Overlap a photo corner, sit in the margins, or point at a button. Three per screen at most.

Watercolour scenes

For grants and projects: what the money made possible. Loose, soft, with a scatter of pink hearts. Always on white.

Children's artwork

Designs drawn by children, like the Christmas jumper. Credit the artist by first name and age, with permission: "Designed by Ava, 7".

Do and don'tKeep it hand-made

The doodles only work because they're real. Don't mix in anything that looks generated, vector or stock.

Brand / Photography

Photography

Real people, real moments, warm light. Pictures you'd stick in a family album, not ones you'd buy from a stock library.

Together Elsie's Squad Silly Faces Wings On Mini Marathon The First Dinner Christmas Elsie's Crew

The directionThree things to look for

If a photo has all three, use it. If it has none, don't.

Candid and joyful

Caught mid-laugh, mid-run, mid-hug. Kids being kids. Never posed in a line staring at the camera.

Warm and soft

Natural light, blush and cream tones, a touch of film softness. No harsh flash or heavy filters.

Community in action

People doing something together: walking, running, making, celebrating. Show the "action" in love, legacy and action.

FramingShow photos like a scrapbook

Put photos in a polaroid with a pen caption, or lay them loose with a short shadow. Never full-bleed with text over the top.

Consent first: only use photos of children with written permission from their parents or guardians, and never show anyone in distress.

Brand / Tone of Voice

Tone of Voice

We speak like Elsie's family and friends would: warm, honest and hopeful. Three principles, used together, in everything we write.

Principle oneWarm, never sad

  • Lead with love, not loss. Talk about what's growing, not only what's gone.
  • Be gentle with grief. Never use Elsie's story to make people feel guilty.
  • Sign off with love. "With love, Jen & David" beats "Kind regards".
Less this

Every day, families face heartbreaking tragedy. Without your donation, they'll have nowhere to turn.

More this

Every gift helps a family feel a little less alone.

Principle twoPersonal, never corporate

  • Say "we", "you" and "Elsie". Real names, real people.
  • Write like you'd talk to a friend over a cuppa.
  • Skip the jargon. No "beneficiaries", "stakeholders" or "impactful".
Less this

The organisation provides grant funding to eligible beneficiaries across the region.

More this

We fund the things families need most.

Principle threeHopeful and active

  • Give one clear next step, with a verb: Donate Now, Apply Now, Make It Happen.
  • Show what changed. A garden built, a family helped, a mile run.
  • Invite, don't plead. "Be part of the story."
Less this

Please consider supporting our cause if you are able to.

More this

Take on a challenge. We'll be with you every step.
